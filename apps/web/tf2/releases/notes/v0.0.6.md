# playsrc v0.0.6

The map just got a lot bigger.

This update brings `pl_upward` into the browser alongside `jump_beef`, rebuilds a substantial part of the Source rendering and loading pipeline, makes mouse movement noticeably smoother, and fixes the rocket effects that kept disappearing behind the walls they had just hit.

Both maps now ship through the same release, the same content catalog, and the same TF2 WebAssembly runtime. The result is a much more complete view of a real Source map, with less waiting, less memory pressure, and considerably faster frames.

## World and Maps

### pl_upward Arrives

`pl_upward` is now available as a second declared browser target alongside `jump_beef`.

This is the actual configured TF2 map, loaded from its original BSP and Source content dependencies rather than from a simplified recreation or exported scene. You can load the map, inspect its world geometry, and move through it using the existing local noclip mode.

The map now includes:

- All 558 authored displacement terrain patches.
- All 1,244 authored static props, including 1,184 props in the main world and 60 props belonging to the 3D sky.
- 234 distinct static-prop model identities.
- The complete authored 3D sky, including 548 sky-owned surfaces.
- 554 static-prop physics collision objects.
- 2,480 exact static-prop vertex-lighting objects.
- Four authored boulders that receive runtime world lighting instead of substituted baked colors.

`jump_beef` remains available as the default map, and both maps can be selected from the same browser release without swapping builds or replacing the shared game runtime.

### Displacement Terrain

Terrain now preserves the original Source displacement topology instead of behaving like an ordinary flat brush surface.

The displacement pipeline retains authored positions, face ownership, UVs, lightmap coordinates, vertex alpha, allowed-vertex information, and Source-facing triangle orientation. Incorrect winding and inverted surface normals have been corrected, so terrain renders from the proper side and responds to lighting consistently.

Collision now uses the complete displacement grid:

- 558 collision patches.
- 14,174 collision positions.
- 18,240 collision triangles.

Point traces, rays, swept hulls, stationary overlap, displacement identities, and deterministic hit ordering all use the same authored terrain instead of a separate visual-only approximation.

### Authored 3D Sky

`pl_upward` now renders its original Source 3D sky as an independent view.

Sky geometry and sky-owned props use their own scaled camera, visibility area, fog, and draw order. The main world resumes only after the sky depth buffer is reset and the previous rendering state has been restored.

This means distant sky structures remain in their correct authored space rather than being mixed into the main world, omitted entirely, or replaced with a flat background.

### Static Props and Lighting

Static props now retain their original model occurrence, transform, leaf membership, skin, fade settings, and lighting ownership.

Baked vertex lighting is read from the actual Source VHV data. Runtime-lit props sample the surrounding world instead of falling back to arbitrary colors, and their lighting records remain distinct from their otherwise identical model templates.

The renderer also preserves the split between main-world props and 3D-sky props. A sky-owned prop can no longer accidentally appear in the playable world, and main-world props cannot leak into the sky pass.

## Rendering and Visual Effects

### Rocket Trails and Explosions Are Visible Again

Rocket smoke, explosion flashes, debris, and late smoke now appear in front of the wall they actually hit.

Previously, the WebGPU backend recorded opaque world geometry into render bundles but executed those bundles after transparent particle draws. The particles were in the correct physical location, but the wall was drawn over them afterward, making the explosion look buried behind the level.

World bundles now execute during the correct opaque phase, before transparent rocket effects. The fix preserves real world-depth testing: particles in front of the impacted wall remain visible, while particles behind another solid wall remain hidden.

Controlled headed-browser captures now show:

- 3,617 changed pixels on the impacted wall surface.
- 1,586 visible warm explosion-flash pixels.
- 4,229 late smoke and debris pixels.
- Zero leaked particle pixels across five nearer-wall occlusion controls.

Rocket trails continue to follow the original model's `trail` attachment, and stopping the trail no longer removes smoke particles that were already emitted.

### Correct Source Particle Orientation

Particle effects now use the actual Source forward, right, and up basis when transforming authored local velocities.

A handedness mismatch previously treated Source's right axis like the opposite matrix column. That affected the direction of wall-explosion smoke, debris, and other child effects even when their parent control point and collision normal were correct.

Wall explosions now preserve the original impact normal, the exact one-unit offset away from the collision plane, the configured effect orientation, and the authored order of their child systems.

Invalid solid-impact normals now fail atomically instead of being replaced with an invented upward direction.

### More Efficient World Rendering

The renderer no longer rebuilds the entire visible world on every ordinary frame.

World-face ownership, static-prop leaf membership, immutable scene bounds, main-world visibility, and 3D-sky visibility are retained independently. Only the parts of the scene affected by a real view or visibility change are updated.

Particle geometry is now prepared without repeatedly allocating temporary Three.js vectors, while static scene transforms and world bundles remain reusable across unchanged frames.

Separate world, sky, particle, and first-person weapon passes still retain their original ordering and depth behavior.

## Performance and Responsiveness

### Faster Frames

The combined release was measured in a visibly headed Chromium browser at 1280 by 720 on an Apple M4, with gameplay, HUD, world rendering, particles, audio, and GPU submission active.

Measured full-frame results after all optimizations were combined:

| Scenario | Median frame time | 95th percentile | 99th percentile |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Repeated jumping | 0.565 ms | 0.640 ms | 0.750 ms |
| Continuous movement | 0.515 ms | 0.610 ms | 1.005 ms |
| Sustained rocket fire | 0.535 ms | 0.960 ms | 1.290 ms |

Before the renderer rewrite, the repeated-jump scenario reached 7.180 ms at the 95th percentile. The completed renderer change reduced that isolated scenario to 0.775 ms, and the final combined release measured 0.640 ms.

Continuous movement improved from 3.080 ms to 0.770 ms in the isolated renderer comparison, then reached 0.610 ms after the remaining improvements were integrated.

Sustained rocket fire improved from 2.565 ms to 1.075 ms in the isolated renderer comparison and reached 0.960 ms in the final combined release, with visible rocket smoke and explosions still enabled.

These measurements do not lower visual quality, remove props, reduce map geometry, skip particle systems, or slow the simulation.

### Smoother Mouse Look

Mouse movement is no longer tied to the arrival of a newly prepared simulation frame.

The browser now samples the latest look direction independently and can redraw an existing authoritative frame when the view changes. This preserves the actual Source-style distinction between rendering the current view and advancing fixed-tick gameplay.

In the same continuous-look scenario:

- The previous implementation displayed 48 look frames.
- The new implementation displayed 85 look frames.
- 36 of those frames independently updated the current view without requiring a new prepared simulation snapshot.
- All 149 measured mouse-input events were retained.
- Gameplay continued at approximately 66.6 simulation ticks per second.

This is not a mouse-polling-rate change. The improvement comes from better frame handoff, lower main-thread work, and allowing presentation to keep up with the current input state.

### Fewer Worker Round Trips

The TF2 browser Worker now batches compatible operations instead of sending a separate message for every small phase.

In the fixed headed-browser comparison:

- Total Worker requests dropped from 12,536 to approximately 9,415.
- Standalone visibility requests dropped from 3,157 to 36 or 37.
- Map staging dropped from four Worker requests to one.
- Model request time at the 95th percentile improved from 1.335 ms to approximately 1.060-1.105 ms.

Visibility is still calculated whenever the actual view requires it. The difference is that model preparation and its matching visibility work now cross the Worker boundary together instead of paying the messaging and scheduling cost twice.

### Faster Map Compilation and Lower Memory Use

Map loading now avoids repeated visibility construction, duplicate resource hashing, unnecessary material decoding, repeated lighting calculations, and oversized temporary output buffers.

Measured headed-browser medians:

- Cold map selection to complete Ready state improved from 4,383.1 ms to 2,949.6 ms.
- Cold Worker loading improved from 2,272.5 ms to 1,357.9 ms.
- Verified visibility and environment preparation improved from 96.28 ms to 31.47 ms.
- One cold texture-hashing phase dropped from 175.495 ms to approximately 2-3 ms by reusing already verified resource hashes.

For `pl_upward`, the presentation compiler no longer reserves a full 512 MiB buffer for an artifact that needs approximately 131 MiB. That removes 405,502,419 bytes of unnecessary reserved space during compilation.

The exact map outputs, content hashes, authored textures, static props, displacement geometry, and scene-activation boundary remain unchanged.

### Faster Source Visibility Queries

Potentially visible sets, potentially audible sets, BSP leaves, and ancestor-node relationships are now decoded and indexed once instead of being repeatedly reconstructed during view queries.

The low-level native visibility kernel improved from 12.125 microseconds to 0.666 microseconds on `jump_beef`, and from 45.667 microseconds to 0.875 microseconds on `pl_upward`.

Those are isolated owner-level diagnostics, not browser frame claims. In the actual headed browser, the visibility WebAssembly transaction improved from 0.260 ms to 0.185 ms at the 95th percentile before the broader Worker batching changes were combined.

Area portals, outside-world behavior, camera-side traversal order, sky ownership, and exact visible-surface identities remain intact.

## Gameplay and HUD

### Rocket Collision and Impact Effects

Stock rockets retain their actual Source collision normal and place their explosion exactly one unit away from the impacted surface.

Wall impacts now preserve the correct event order, contact orientation, authored wall-versus-midair effect selection, explosion flash, debris, smoke lifetime, and natural cleanup.

The verifier now checks the actual pixels on the impacted wall instead of accepting an explosion simply because a particle name appeared in a diagnostic record.

### HUD Carrying Panel Fix

The large grey outlined panel showing `[unknown]` at the bottom of the screen has been removed from normal gameplay.

That panel is an authentic TF2 interface element intended for weapons dropped by another player. It was incorrectly left visible while holding a normal locally owned stock weapon, even though its carrying-name variable was never populated.

The HUD now applies the real Source ownership rule and keeps the entire carrying panel hidden when it does not belong on screen.

### Ammo Number Shadow Fix

Ammo digits no longer look like two bright numbers drawn on top of each other.

TF2 intentionally draws a foreground label and a one-pixel-offset shadow label for clip and reserve ammunition. The browser had ignored the authored label foreground colors, causing both layers to inherit the same bright color.

The HUD now preserves the actual configured colors:

- Clip and reserve digits use `TanLight`.
- The clip shadow uses `Black`.
- The reserve shadow uses `TransparentBlack`.

The original shadow layers remain present; they now read as shadows instead of duplicate text.

### More Accurate Source Entity Values

TF2 entity fields now follow the numeric conversion behavior used by their actual Source data-map consumers.

Team filters, class filters, negation flags, bodygroup values, disabled states, push speeds, and catapult speeds retain Source's numeric-prefix handling without rewriting the original entity bytes or weakening semantic validation.

## Loading, Packaging, and Delivery

### One Release for Both Maps

`jump_beef` and `pl_upward` now share one immutable TF2 release descriptor, one resource catalog, and one WebAssembly build.

The catalog records each map's exact BSP, Source resource graph, and dependency ledger independently. Selecting one map cannot accidentally substitute resources or object identities from the other.

Map replacement consumes one-shot input exactly once, resets the correct generation-owned state, and keeps the previous valid experience alive until the replacement is ready to activate.

### Authored Release Notes

Releases now include a reviewed, version-specific changelog instead of GitHub's autogenerated pull-request list.

Every release remains a sequential patch release. The release workflow validates the exact versioned changelog before creating its GitHub Release.

## Additional Fixes

- Corrected Source static-prop game-lump parsing for the layouts used by both configured maps.
- Preserved exact displacement-facing orientation, surface normals, and lightmap association.
- Removed duplicate model-texture materialization during map preparation.
- Added bounded collision-lighting ray batches and complete world-surface lighting queries.
- Restored correct static-prop vertex-lighting artifact identities and production packaging.
- Prevented stale renderer results from publishing after cancellation or map replacement.
- Suspended hidden GameUI, loading, HUD, and Options frame owners instead of advancing them unnecessarily.
- Indexed configured physical input bindings instead of rebuilding complete settings snapshots for every key or mouse event.
- Replaced repeated whole-application UI reconciliation with incremental publication of unchanged gameplay observations.

## Current Scope

`pl_upward` is available for local map inspection and noclip movement. Payload carts, checkpoints, round timers, teams, scoring, overtime, and win conditions are not implemented in this release.

Demoman sticky projectile gameplay remains blocked on the exact Source VPhysics implementation. Online multiplayer, hosted matches, and additional maps are not part of the current release.
