# playsrc v0.0.10

This update restores the view through the Sniper scope, corrects model lighting and moving doors, adds authored Spy concealment and class-menu presentation, and removes several measured sources of browser freezing.

The release combines rendering, loading, memory, and local-bot improvements across `jump_beef`, `pl_upward`, and `ctf_2fort`. It is an incremental update, not a claim of stable 60 FPS on every supported scene: the final Windows checks still expose lower steady gameplay cadence and occasional class/menu and first-use effect stalls. Those limitations are detailed below.

## World and Maps

### Doors Move Visibly With Their Collision

Triggered doors on 2Fort now visibly slide or rise with their authoritative movers. Previously, a parented door's collision and CPU transform could move while the GPU continued drawing it closed. The renderer now refreshes the changed occurrence beneath the retained world root, without drawing another door or rebuilding the map.

Upward's animated doors use their authored model sequences rather than a timer inferred from brush speed. Native movement checks cover passage, closing, reopening, and both 2Fort teams, with same-frame pixels and depth witnesses. `jump_beef` keeps its authored damage-button behavior rather than acquiring invented proximity triggers.

Related pull request: [#192](https://github.com/Hona/playsrc/pull/192).

### Water From Elevated Views

Looking straight down at `jump_beef` water no longer loses its authored surface. Water batches remain outside immutable world bundles so each view retains control over surface visibility and material selection. Fifteen elevated angle, altitude, and lateral pixel comparisons preserve opaque depth and the configured visibility set.

### Exact Collision With Less Repeated Work

Bot movement and player hull queries retain collision candidates, transformed convex support data, and displacement bounds instead of repeatedly rebuilding or projecting unchanged geometry. Queries still consume authored ordering and preserve first-hit ties, start-solid results, surface identities, normals, and floating-point results.

The configured Upward displacement comparison covers 558 patches, 18,240 triangles, and 145,920 traces with exact field and bit matches. Its local query loop falls from 259.020 to 176.503 ms. Separate convex command replay reduces aggregate observation work from 997.268 to 874.437 ms while matching all 1,038 ticks and 870 publications. These are controlled CPU/WASM results, not end-to-end browser FPS gains.

## Loading, Packaging, and Delivery

### Authenticated Warm Loads

One bounded IndexedDB content-addressed store owns persistent immutable resources. Cache hits update small recency records rather than rewriting full Blobs or scanning the inventory. Newly authenticated Blobs and matching metadata commit atomically; intact verified-at-write records avoid another full-payload hash. Torn or corrupt records are rejected and recovered. This integrity boundary assumes the application origin itself is trusted.

An eight-transfer scheduler prioritizes critical objects, deduplicates in-flight requests, and retains independent cancellation. Startup overlaps catalog, resource, WASM, cache opening, and compilation. The loading watchdog observes real received bytes and suspends its remaining visible-time budget while the tab is hidden.

Warm reloads reuse authenticated canonical identities, and bounded parallel resource/BSP archive decoding avoids redundant hashing and copies. The earlier same-context Upward cache comparison reduces warm wire traffic from 494,897,725 to 12,774,035 bytes with zero immutable requests. A later local 1280x720 headed decode check reaches first playable frames in 5.955 seconds for warm 15-bot Upward and 5.859 seconds for warm 23-bot 2Fort. Those local stage measurements are not production-network or final Windows loading guarantees.

## Additional Fixes

- Profiling separates animation callbacks, application-completed frames, GPU submissions, compositor events, and proven content identities. A compositor repeating an old surface is not reported as a new gameplay frame.
- Bounded native traces retain failures and sample-boundary stalls. Offline analysis verifies hashes and clocks, keeps unknown causes unknown, and separates collection-only gaps from active gameplay.
- Worker CPU attribution exposes actual catch-up work without inferring a blocker from nearby events. The earlier discarded half-second trace is not retroactively claimed solved.
- Native Windows capture waits for the Worker execution context before evaluating instrumentation, avoiding the observed pre-menu renderer breakpoint crash.
- Checked FIFO profiling leases, exact reusable builds, and bounded cleanup reduce diagnostic waiting without changing gameplay or measurement clocks.

## Current Scope

- Stable 60+ completed gameplay FPS without periodic freezes remains a target, not a completed release guarantee. Remaining work includes steady publication-to-presentation cadence, class/menu latency, and first-use particle and animated-map-prop programs.
- Gameplay remains local single-player and bots. Human networked multiplayer and Source 2 are not included.
- 2Fort retains local 24-player RED-versus-BLU Capture the Flag. Upward retains authored world, entities, bots, combat, round state, and navigation, but not the complete physics-driven Payload cart and constraint behavior.
- Demoman's Bottle is playable. Stickybomb Launcher gameplay and complete pipe-grenade trajectories remain unavailable pending the exact rigid-body solver; unfinished VPhysics work is excluded.
- Engineer stock buildings, placement rules, construction/destruction menus, and wrench upgrades remain available.
- Browser WebGPU does not expose application-controlled display synchronization; VSync remains capability-gated.
- Only `jump_beef`, `pl_upward`, and `ctf_2fort` are configured release maps. `jump_beef` runs without bots because it has no configured authored navigation mesh.
